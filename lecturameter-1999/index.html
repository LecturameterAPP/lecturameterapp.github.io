Lecturameter 99: Reading Log

Lecturameter as if you had installed it from a CD-ROM in 1999. A standalone app, with windows, menus and tables, that opens your real backups.

Formerly Lecturameter 1999. The stores may still list it under that name: it is the same app.

What it is

A late-nineties desktop program running on your phone. One window over a teal desktop, a menu bar, a toolbar, a folder tree, a dense table and a status bar.

  • Library tree: all books, reading, pending, finished, dropped and by author
  • Table with title, author, status, pages, sessions, time and last read. Tap a column header to sort
  • Real book search, using the same catalog Lecturameter uses
  • Imports and exports the Lecturameter backup and the Goodreads CSV. Your data goes in and out without losing anything

Why it exists

An experiment: rebuild the idea of Lecturameter from scratch under radically different design premises, without touching the real app. Of all the reinterpretations, the 1999 one was the one I liked most, so I finished it as a fun exercise and turned it into a complete client. It is simpler than Lecturameter on purpose: it is an experiment and, in principle, it will not get any further development.

  • Timer in a floating window with a green LCD display. It moves, minimizes to a taskbar and keeps counting in the status bar
  • When you stop, the save session dialog lets you log it by pages, by percentage or by time only, and works out your pace just like Lecturameter
  • Saved sessions are Lecturameter sessions: they come back to the modern app if you export a Lecturameter 99 backup and import it in Lecturameter.

Reading report

Statistics are a printed report in monospaced text: summary, time per book with block bars and speed in pages per day.

Year calendar

Every day of the year is drawn as one character depending on the pages you read, with the last seven days below in block bars.

Session history

Sessions grouped by book in a tree with plus and minus, with five figures at the top: sessions, total time, pages, pages per minute and pages per day.

Themes

Classic 98 is free and the default. The pack adds six more on Android and four on iPhone, each with its own background and its own sounds.

Pick it from the View menu

View, Theme... opens a window with the themes: seven on Android and five on iPhone. The change reaches every window, the timer and Pagi at once.

  • Classic 98, free and the default: grey windows, navy title bars and a teal desktop
  • Theme pack: 1984, Gumdrop, Memory, Towers, Core and 8-bit. On iPhone, without 1984 and Gumdrop
  • Each theme brings its own desktop background and its own sounds for taps, windows and the timer
  • View, Theme sounds turns the sounds off if you read in silence

Classic 98

Grey windows, navy title bars and a teal desktop. Free, it is the one the app opens with.

1984

A one-bit black and white desktop, with dot patterns and square windows. Android only.

Gumdrop

Translucent coloured plastic, rounded buttons and stripes in the background. Android only.

Memory

The menus of a nineties disc console, in dark blues.

Towers

Another nineties disc console, night blue with light blue lines.

Core

A black and green console, like a screen that never switches off.

8-bit

Fat pixels, few colours and blocky letters.

Try it before you pay

Tap a locked theme and the whole app changes to it. If you close without buying, the previous theme comes back.

Screensavers

They only appear while the timer is running and you leave the phone alone. They wait 1, 3 or 5 minutes, or never. One tap takes you back and the timer keeps counting.

Flying books

Pixel books flapping their covers across a black screen.

Letter field

Letters from many alphabets drifting past, like a starfield.

Page pipes

Pipes made of pages that draw themselves until the screen fills up.

Bouncing Pagi

Pagi bouncing off the edges of the screen.

Reading clock

The session time in big LCD digits.

Pagi 99 and its costumes

Pagi travels to 1999 too: pixelated and loose on the screen, like the office assistants of the time. You drag it, it talks when you tap it and it shows the tips in yellow balloons. It has eight costumes, chosen in View, Pagi's costume..., and you can try them on before paying.

  • Cassette player
  • Backwards cap
  • 3D glasses
  • Neon sunglasses
  • Floppy disk in hand
  • Leather jacket
  • Pocket pet
  • Cardboard crown

What changes compared to Lecturameter

Same as Lecturameter

  • Same data: books, sessions, statuses, ratings and notes. The backup file is the same in both apps.
  • Book search by title or author.
  • Real timer: it keeps counting in the background and even if you close the app.
  • No account and no ads. It only uses the internet to search for books and, if you leave them on, for pseudonymous usage statistics and crash reports (you can turn them off in Help, Privacy).
  • Available in 50 languages.

Only in the 1999 edition

  • A 1999 desktop interface. No Material.
  • Real windows: you drag them, minimize them and close them with the cross.
  • Reading report in text, with a calendar drawn in characters.
  • Context menu on long press and keyboard shortcuts if you connect a keyboard.
  • Pixelated Pagi 99 with tip balloons.
  • Optional themes with their own background and sounds, screensavers while the timer runs and costumes for Pagi.

What it leaves out, on purpose

  • No Pro and no subscription. Logging your reading is all free, the store only sells looks.
  • No challenges, bingos, yearly wrapped or widgets.
  • No immersive session, ambient sounds or Google Drive backup. The backup is saved as a file.
