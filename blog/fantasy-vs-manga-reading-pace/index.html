Fantasy vs manga: two completely different reading paces

A real dataset of 64 tracked sessions. Fantasy averages 0.63 pages per minute. Manga hits 3.44. What that means when you plan your reading month.

The gap

When people talk about reading pace they say things like "I read about a book a week". That is fine as a mood but useless as a plan, because a book a week for a manga reader and a book a week for a Sanderson reader are two universes.

I have numbers now. Me, four months, 64 tracked reading sessions.

0.63 – 0.95Fantasy prose · pages / min
3.44Manga · pages / min

You do not need statistics for that gap to matter. Manga sessions are roughly five times faster than fantasy sessions, per page. Which means:

  • Same reader (me), same 30 minutes.
  • A fantasy session covers around 20 pages.
  • A manga session covers around 100.

The books I tracked

The Dragon Reborn, book three of The Wheel of Time. 672 pages. 37 tracked sessions across 95 days. Average pace: 0.63 pages per minute. The five slowest sessions I recorded this year are all from this book.

Hábito y mortaja, book two of a Spanish fantasy series. 450 tracked pages, 16 sessions. Average pace: 0.95 pages per minute. Faster prose, tighter chapters. You feel it in the pace.

Frieren, Volume 1, manga. 165 pages, 2 sessions. Average pace: 3.44 pages per minute. The whole volume goes in less than an hour of real reading.

Chainsaw Man tomos 21 to 24, four manga volumes. Every one of them started and finished the same day. Same for Dan Da Dan tomos 18 through 23. Six volumes, six same-day reads.

Why this matters

When you plan a reading month, use the pace of the genre you are actually planning to read.

If your next book is 800 pages of fantasy and you have 45 minutes a day, that is 45 x 0.8 pages per minute, so around 36 pages a day. Around three weeks and a half. This is real information you can use to determine what and how to read next.

If your next book is a 200 page manga volume, that is one Sunday afternoon.

What Lecturameter does with this

The app remembers your average pace per book, not just overall. When you open the book screen you see "at your current pace, X hours to finish". The number is honest, because it is based on how you actually read that book, not a global average that mixes manga and Sanderson.

Any tracker that gives you one single reading speed number is going to lie to you the moment you switch genres. What you want is a per-book pace and a per-session log, so you can spot the pattern.
