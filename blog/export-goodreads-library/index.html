How to export your Goodreads library and take it with you

The steps to export your Goodreads library as CSV, what fields you get, and what to do with the file next. Works on desktop, not mobile.

If you have ever wanted to leave Goodreads and take your library with you, the good news is that Goodreads still lets you do it. The less good news is that the option is hidden and only works on the web.

Here is how to get your CSV, what is inside it, and what you can do with it.

Step by step

The export only works on the desktop site. On mobile the option is missing.

  1. Go to goodreads.com and log in.
  2. Click "My Books" in the top nav.
  3. On the left sidebar, under "Tools", click "Import and export".
  4. On the page that opens, look for the section "Export your books".
  5. Click "Export Library".
  6. Wait a minute. Goodreads generates the file in the background. When it is ready you see a "Your export from Goodreads" link that downloads a CSV.

That is it. The file is called something like goodreads_library_export.csv.

What is in the file

The CSV has one row per book and around 30 columns. The ones that matter:

  • Book Id
  • Title
  • Author
  • Additional Authors
  • ISBN and ISBN13
  • My Rating (0 to 5)
  • Average Rating
  • Publisher
  • Number of Pages
  • Year Published
  • Original Publication Year
  • Date Read
  • Date Added
  • Bookshelves (your custom shelves)
  • Exclusive Shelf (read, currently-reading, to-read)
  • My Review (your text review)
  • Read Count
  • Owned Copies

If you have private notes on Goodreads, those come across in the "Private Notes" column, which is easy to miss.

The rating uses whole stars, not half stars. If Goodreads shows you three and a half stars for a book, the CSV will still say 3.

The dates are in Goodreads format, which is "YYYY/MM/DD" for most locales. Any decent tool will parse it.

What to do with the file

Once you have the CSV you can:

Import it into another reading app. Lecturameter reads Goodreads exports directly, including ratings, dates and shelves. Some other apps do it too, with different rules about what they keep.

Open it in a spreadsheet. If what you want is just to see your list of books outside of Goodreads, a spreadsheet is enough. Google Sheets, LibreOffice Calc and Excel all open a CSV natively.

Keep it as a backup. The file is small. Tuck it somewhere safe. If your Goodreads account ever gets locked or the service disappears, you still have your list.

Convert it to a different format. If you want to move to Bookwyrm, Hardcover, StoryGraph or any other, most of them import Goodreads CSVs or have their own converter.

What Goodreads does not export

The CSV does not include:

  • Your friends
  • Comments on your reviews
  • Likes and votes
  • Groups you belong to
  • Recommendations
  • Progress updates on individual books

Everything you wrote yourself and everything about the books is in the file. The social layer stays on Goodreads.

One thing to know

If your library has thousands of books, sometimes the export button returns a very small file or a broken one. This is a known Goodreads bug that shows up in the help forums every few months. The workaround is to try again a few hours later, ideally at a low-traffic time. Once the CSV downloads properly, it is usually complete.

Then what

If you got the CSV to bring your library to Lecturameter, the import lives in Settings, Import. Lecturameter reads the shelves and asks you which ones to bring across. Ratings, dates and private notes come with the books. Nothing goes to a server. Your library ends up on your phone.
